Why it exists

Capture software is built for recording. This is built for playing.

Every capture utility shows you a preview as an afterthought: stereo, SDR, a frame or two late, in a window that fights your desktop. NitLink is only a viewer, so every decision goes to the picture and the delay between the console and your eyes.

01

Present on arrival

Each captured frame is presented the instant it lands instead of waiting for the next refresh. Tearing-allowed, capped just under your display's refresh, so a VRR panel absorbs it.

02

Real HDR10

The card is asked for 10-bit P010 and the swap chain is HDR10 PQ. Zero color conversion between the console and the panel. Auto-detected on the 4K Pro and 4K X.

03

VRR that means it

Cards deliver a fixed 60 or 120 frames. A GPU frame differ finds the frames that actually changed and presents only those, so G-Sync and FreeSync follow the game, not the card.

Measured, not claimed

Ahead or even on every card tested. Never loses.

PCIe · HDMI 2.1

4K60 HDR10 with VRR passthrough. HDR auto-detect and source mode read straight from the driver. The lowest-latency card tested.

Elgato 4K X

USB · HDMI 2.1

4K120 capture. Native support through the card's own control interface: source name, resolution, HDR state, live source-follow.

Questions people ask

Straight answers.

Does it add lag compared to plugging the console into the monitor?

The capture card adds its own latency and NitLink adds almost none on top: present-to-glass is under a millisecond. Through the 4K Pro the whole chain feels like a direct connection for everything short of frame-perfect competitive play. Numbers above.

Can OBS record while I play in NitLink?

Yes. A capture card can only be opened by one app at a time, so don't point OBS at the card. Add NitLink as a Game Capture source in OBS and record the window; it adds nothing to your play latency.

Surround sound?

No capture card from Elgato forwards more than stereo PCM to the PC, so NitLink plays two channels. Set the console to Linear PCM 2.0 so the center channel isn't lost in the card's downmix.

How heavy is it on the CPU?

About half of one core at 4K60. Nothing is computed on the CPU; the pixel work is on the GPU, and the CPU's job is copying each frame from the driver to the GPU. It doesn't fight a game for cores.

Windows SmartScreen warns about the download.

The binary isn't code-signed yet. Click "More info", then "Run anyway". The source is public and every release is built from a tagged commit.

Is it really free?

Free and MIT-licensed, no telemetry, no account. If it saved you a second monitor, there's a Ko-fi in the app's About panel.
